# Human Layer

**A user-owned computational extension of a human life.**

Human Layer is the personal side of the Agency Layer project.

The core idea is simple:

> **Your digital life already produces a model of you. That model should belong to you.**

Today, fragments of a person are distributed across AI chats, search history, calendars, messages, files, social platforms, purchases, photos, browsing, work, public posts, and other systems. Those fragments are usually interpreted by systems the person cannot fully inspect, correct, or carry elsewhere.

Human Layer flips that relationship.

It creates a private, portable **Sphere**: a living model of the person's reality that they can inspect, question, challenge, correct, and use with any intelligence they choose.

## The emotional product

The user should be able to open the Sphere and feel:

**This is mine.  
I can see what it thinks.  
I can ask why.  
I can correct it.  
I can carry it with me.**

The Sphere is not a claim that a machine "knows the real you."

It is an evidence-backed model that remains subordinate to the human.

## Product loop

```text
digital evidence
    ↓
structured reality model
    ↓
dialogue with the human
    ↓
correction / context / disagreement
    ↓
simulations and decisions
    ↓
real-world action
    ↓
outcomes
    ↓
updated reality model
```

## Core principles

1. **The human owns the Sphere.**
2. **Every important claim can show its evidence.**
3. **Fact, inference, interpretation, and self-assertion remain distinct.**
4. **The human has a right of reply to their own model.**
5. **The model can challenge the human, but it cannot silently redefine them.**
6. **External reality still outranks both the human and the model.**
7. **The Sphere must be portable across model providers.**
8. **Access is scoped. Other AIs should receive only the slice they need.**
9. **Corrections are first-class data.**
10. **The product should reduce cognitive load, not create a second life to maintain.**

## First wedge

Do not try to ingest the entire internet on day one.

Start with the sources that already contain unusually rich first-person context:

- AI conversation exports
- notes and documents
- calendar
- email
- browser history
- public web footprint
- manually imported files
- structured user corrections

The first magic moment is not "we imported everything."

It is:

> **"Holy shit. That actually understands why I did that — and when it was wrong, I could fix it."**

## The simplest promise

**See your reality. Own your model. Shape what comes next.**
